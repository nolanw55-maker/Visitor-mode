What it said

    Nothing yet. Press Play demo to start.

    When it speaks

    Setting off
    The first time the car moves, it states the rule for the country.
    Pulling away
    After any stop of 2 seconds or more: “Keep left.” No more than once every 30 seconds, so queues don't nag.
    Stopped
    2.5 seconds after stopping at the end of a real stretch of driving (60 m or more): “Look right first.” Once per stop, at most every 45 seconds.
    After a turn
    When the heading swings 55° or more within 12 seconds, straightens out, and the car drives on above about 9 mph: “Keep left.” Skipped if it has spoken in the last 15 seconds.
    After a break
    A stop of 2 minutes or more (fuel, a car park, a view) gets a longer reminder as you pull away.
    Side check
    Live drive only, experimental. Against the flow on a one-way street, dual carriageway or roundabout for 5 seconds: “Wrong way.” On a two-way road, if the GPS puts you more than 1.5 m over the centre line for 8 seconds (median of the last 15 fixes): “You may be on the wrong side.”
    At night
    Once the sun is 4° below the horizon, worked out from your position and the time, a reminder after every few minutes of driving with nothing else said.

    The phrases flip automatically when you're visiting a country that drives on the right.

    What it can't do yet

    • It has no map, so it can't warn you before a junction or roundabout, and it can't tell a junction from a queue or a petrol pump. Sometimes it will say “look right” when you didn't need it.
    • The side check works from GPS and map data. Direction on one-way roads should be dependable. On two-way roads phone GPS is often only good to 3 to 5 metres, so it can miss you or warn when you're fine, especially between tall buildings. Doing this properly needs a camera that sees the road markings.
    • Live mode needs the screen on and this page open.
    Prototype, free to copy and adapt. Set it up before you set off and keep the phone in a holder. It reminds you at risky moments; it doesn't watch the road or replace your own attention.
